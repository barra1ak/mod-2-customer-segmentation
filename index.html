Step 1 of 4 Learn the territory lenses

Step 1: Use seven territory lenses

A territory is more than a list of accounts. Strong analysis connects the physical market, production realities, customer structure, competitors, trends, and outside forces.

Your assignment: Investigate the fictional Heartland East territory. Review each zone, classify the evidence, build a priority map, and recommend a territory strategy.
GEO

Geography

Distance, transportation, terrain, service coverage, and physical market boundaries.

PS

Production systems

Crop and livestock systems, operating scale, seasonality, and production practices.

TR

Trends

Demand shifts, consolidation, labor pressure, margin changes, and emerging needs.

REG

Regulation

Rules, incentives, reporting requirements, and compliance pressures.

TECH

Technology

Adoption levels, infrastructure, interoperability, and technical readiness.

COMP

Competition

Competitor strength, channel access, local loyalty, price pressure, and white space.

CC

Customer concentration

Whether opportunity is spread across many accounts or concentrated in a few powerful customers.

Step 2: Investigate the territory zones

Select each zone on the map. Review the evidence, classify the zone’s dominant opportunity type, and explain which territory signals shaped your conclusion.

Heartland East territory
Tip: High market size does not always mean high priority. Look for the combination of customer need, competitive openness, readiness, and practical coverage.

Step 3: Build the territory priority map

Your matrix summarizes what you learned. Now choose one primary zone and one secondary zone, then test a territory hypothesis.

Step 4: Review the territory coaching

Territory strategy is a hypothesis, not a permanent answer. Good analysis makes the reasoning explicit and identifies what should be monitored next.
