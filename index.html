Step 1: Learn the three segmentation lenses

A market segment is a group of customers who may respond similarly to an offer because they share important needs, goals, behaviors, or beliefs about what creates value.

Your assignment: Learn the three textbook segmentation methods, practice recognizing them, build one customer segment, and test whether it is useful enough to guide action.
DEM

Demographics

Observable characteristics such as operation size, age, income, education, acreage, or herd size.

USE

Product usage

What customers grow, raise, use, manage, or experience in their operation.

PSY

Psychographics

Buying behavior and value structure—how customers make decisions and what they believe matters most.

Step 2: Identify the segmentation method

Read each market description and choose the textbook segmentation method being used.

Step 3: Build a customer segment

First, work through a simple example. Then use the same three-part process with the customer files: choose one lens, find a specific shared trait, and explain why that trait matters.

Let’s do one together

Example: Small livestock operations

Follow the reasoning—not just the final answer.

1

Choose one lens

We choose Demographics because we want to group customers by observable operation size.

2

Find the shared trait

Farm A has 140 dairy cows and Farm B has 175 dairy cows. Both are small livestock operations.

3

Explain why it matters

Smaller operations may value affordable options, simple implementation, and personal support.

A complete answer could be:
“Using the demographic lens, I grouped Farm A and Farm B as Small Livestock Operations because both have relatively small herds. They may respond similarly to an affordable, easy-to-implement program with personal service.”
